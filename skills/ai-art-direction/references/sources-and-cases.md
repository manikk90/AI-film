## Evidence rules

Separate creator statements, institutional records, critical readings, operational interpretations and proposed transfers. Modern Art Direction commentary is attributed to its author; it is not verified creator intent. Full restricted Iris/course entries were not accessed. Definitions inferred from its public preview are working readings, not proprietary equivalents. The colour review is foundational 2015 research, not an exhaustive review through 2026. No audience experiment, model benchmark or film-generation quality test was run for the research.

## Rhetoric framework

Phillips and McQuarrie (S01) propose three structures (juxtaposition, fusion, replacement) crossed with three meaning operations (connection, similarity, opposition), yielding nine proposed configurations for pictorial advertising. This framework is not a guarantee that complexity improves communication. Transfer to film requires task-specific judgment. Iris publicly names form substitution, contextual metaphor, semantic compounding, pop-out and silhouette (S02); its complete restricted taxonomy remains unverified.

## Eight case studies

The source facts below are deliberately brief. Transfers and boundaries are this report's proposed applications, not confirmed creator intentions or measured audience outcomes. Read the linked originals for the actual works.

### Case 1 - Parasite: space can carry the drama

**Source finding:** In the 2019 interview, Bong connects the semi-basement condition with the family's unstable hope; Lee describes researching inhabited traces and arranging the sets around cinematic needs. [S22](sources-and-cases.md#s22)

**Proposed transfer:** map levels, access, sightlines and routes before camera selection. Let the required action be possible in the set. Research ordinary use rather than adding generic clutter.

**Boundary:** this does not mean every poor home must be dark or dirty. Borrow the problem-solving method, not the Korean house, its plot or its cultural coding. No whole-film pixel/shot-by-shot analysis was performed here.

### Case 2 - Her: a future world can be emotionally specific

**Source finding:** The Academy describes the film's near-future visual concept and Barrett's combination of Los Angeles and Shanghai's Pudong skyline. [S23](sources-and-cases.md#s23)

**Proposed transfer:** start with the emotional and social conditions of the future, then choose design changes that support them. A future setting need not default to neon interfaces, glossy metal or hostile blue light.

**Boundary:** those are alternative design possibilities, not claims about every set in the film. Do not import a sci-fi look into a locked contemporary village.

### Case 3 - MIT Media Lab: a system is more than a logo

**Source finding:** Pentagram developed an ML monogram and related marks for 23 research groups from the same seven-by-seven grid used by the earlier identity. The designer's account describes fixed identity alongside variation. [S20](sources-and-cases.md#s20)

**Proposed transfer:** define what stays invariant and what may change across posters, episodes or touchpoints. A repeatable geometry can create family resemblance without requiring identical layouts.

**Boundary:** a consistent series is not proof of comprehension, market performance or originality. Do not copy the actual glyphs into a new brand.

### Case 4 - EYE-BEE-M: recognition depends on language

**Source finding:** UMMA describes Paul Rand's use of an eye and bee to substitute for the sounds/letters I and B, alongside the striped M. Its record concerns a particular edition. [S21](sources-and-cases.md#s21)

**Proposed transfer:** test pictorial wordplay in the intended language. Separate object recognition from the sound/word inference required by the rebus.

**Boundary:** do not assume English soundplay survives translation into Hindi. Also distinguish an edition's collection date from a design's first creation date.

### Case 5 - Oppenheim's Object: material can change function

**Source finding:** MoMA records the 1936 fur-covered cup, saucer and spoon and discusses the conflicting tactile and drinking associations. [S24](sources-and-cases.md#s24)

**Proposed transfer:** ask what a material makes an object invite, resist or suggest. Material choice can do conceptual work even when the silhouette stays familiar.

**Boundary:** this is an art-object example. Replacing a brass production prop's material without permission would be a continuity failure, not conceptual sophistication.

### Case 6 - Mako analysis: study ingredients, not a copied look

**Source finding:** Murray's analysis identifies visible facture, visual quotation, nested framing and size contrasts as recurring ingredients. This is the critic's reading of the work. [S09](sources-and-cases.md#s09)

**Proposed transfer:** annotate a reference by relationship, evidence and attention mechanism. Recombine those mechanisms for a different brief rather than copying a photographer's subject, set and palette together.

**Boundary:** a material-looking AI artefact is not automatically intentional facture. Nor does the analysis independently verify the maker's complete process.

### Case 7 - Polaroid commentary: a small choice can anchor a reading

**Source finding:** Murray interprets blue handwriting in the campaign as a personal, human trace and contrasts it with a typeset treatment. [S10](sources-and-cases.md#s10)

**Proposed transfer:** identify the one execution detail carrying the intended association, then compare whether another treatment changes the message.

**Boundary:** blue ink is not universal proof of authenticity. The critic's contract/ink assertions are not adopted as legal facts or scientific colour rules. No campaign conversion or trust lift was verified.

### Case 8 - Milkbar feature: the medium can continue the world

**Source finding:** The guest feature describes a locally inflected imagined past and a newspaper execution, with founder comments about period printing/paper choices and physical distribution. [S11](sources-and-cases.md#s11)

**Proposed transfer:** an art-direction idea can influence format and distribution, not only a photograph's grade. Test whether the chosen touchpoint makes sense for the audience and concept.

**Boundary:** a reported campaign is not an experiment proving offline promotion works better. Cultural references and nostalgia require local reading, not universal assumptions.
