# Technique catalogue

40 nonexclusive working techniques, not an exhaustive or proprietary taxonomy. Source-supported explanations and proposed applications are distinguished below. Choose by story/communication need; never apply every technique to every shot. V/P/C/M/T identify technique cards; Sxx identifies a source.

## Index
- 1. Meaning and visual rhetoric
- 2. Attention and perceptual grouping
- 3. Space, composition and scale
- 4. Materials, craft and graphic systems
- 5. Narrative and sequence strategies

## 1. Meaning and visual rhetoric

Working explanations below combine established vocabulary and explicit interpretations. V04-V05 are operational readings, not verified full Iris definitions. The academic replacement category is not identical to every physical object swap.

### V01 - Juxtaposition

**Meaning:** Distinct elements shown together invite a relationship. [S01](sources-and-cases.md#s01)

**Choose when:** Show two relevant states for comparison.

**Failure:** Two unrelated attractive objects provide no interpretable connection.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: an approved worn work tool beside its unused pristine counterpart, when that contrast serves the brief.

### V02 - Fusion

**Meaning:** Two recognisable domains share one composite form. [S01](sources-and-cases.md#s01)

**Choose when:** Compress a comparison into a campaign image.

**Failure:** A hybrid can look inventive while obscuring both source concepts.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed conceptual exercise: a calendar whose pages form a protective enclosure; explain the intended protection-of-time relation.

### V03 - Replacement

**Meaning:** One shown element prompts inference of another absent element. [S01](sources-and-cases.md#s01)

**Choose when:** Let context supply a missing concept.

**Failure:** Showing both elements may change the intended structure to juxtaposition or fusion.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: replace an expected component in a familiar setting while keeping enough context for viewers to infer its usual role.

### V04 - Contextual metaphor

**Meaning:** Unexpected use or setting suggests a second meaning; working interpretation. [S02](sources-and-cases.md#s02)

**Choose when:** Make a familiar item carry a new idea.

**Failure:** Random incongruity becomes a puzzle without a useful intended reading.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: a permitted object treated according to another object's ritual of use, only in an explicitly conceptual image.

### V05 - Semantic compounding

**Meaning:** Several meanings operate together; exact restricted Iris entry remains unverified. [S02](sources-and-cases.md#s02)

**Choose when:** Explore a compact image with a purposeful second reading.

**Failure:** Multiple possible readings can defeat the required message.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: write both intended readings first, then check whether the visible evidence supports each independently.

### V06 - Metonymy

**Meaning:** A part or associated element stands for a larger whole. [S01](sources-and-cases.md#s01)

**Choose when:** Communicate through a meaningful existing detail.

**Failure:** A generic detail may identify neither character nor situation.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: an established working hand and tool can imply labour; it cannot identify an unseen person without sufficient context.

### V07 - Rebus

**Meaning:** Pictures substitute for spoken sounds or letters. [S21](sources-and-cases.md#s21)

**Choose when:** Create a language-dependent graphic message.

**Failure:** The wordplay may fail in a different language or culture.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: test a Hindi sound-based design with Hindi readers instead of assuming an English rebus translates.

### V08 - Opposition / visual irony

**Meaning:** Visible elements invite a meaningful difference or contradiction. [S01](sources-and-cases.md#s01)

**Choose when:** Expose a gap between appearance and claim.

**Failure:** The viewer may infer similarity when opposition was intended.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: contrast an authorised claim of order with already established visible disorder; do not invent incriminating evidence.

## 2. Attention and perceptual grouping

The references here largely teach visual/interface design. Their application to a film frame is a proposed transfer. They describe perceptual tendencies, not a guarantee that all viewers interpret an image identically.

### P01 - Visual hierarchy

**Meaning:** Order attention through relative emphasis. [S04](sources-and-cases.md#s04)

**Choose when:** Establish first read and essential secondary evidence.

**Failure:** A large bright background feature can outrank the intended expression.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: judge the frame at delivery size before adding detail to its least important area.

### P02 - Contrast / pop-out

**Meaning:** A visible difference makes an element distinguishable. [S04](sources-and-cases.md#s04)

**Choose when:** Separate a relevant detail through value, colour, size or orientation.

**Failure:** Too many competing differences create several accidental focal points.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: keep one critical gesture legible against an existing quieter background rather than adding a new spotlight.

### P03 - Figure-ground

**Meaning:** Perception organises a subject relative to its surrounding field. [S05](sources-and-cases.md#s05)

**Choose when:** Maintain a readable outline and subject relationship.

**Failure:** Subject and background can merge at the decisive contact or silhouette.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: change an eligible camera position to separate a hand from a similar-value surface without moving its owner.

### P04 - Closure

**Meaning:** A partial shape can suggest a complete object. [S05](sources-and-cases.md#s05)

**Choose when:** Use controlled omission when recognition is sufficient.

**Failure:** Cropping necessary evidence is not purposeful closure.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: test recognisability without showing every contour; retain full information for a required hand transfer.

### P05 - Proximity

**Meaning:** Near elements tend to be read as related. [S07](sources-and-cases.md#s07)

**Choose when:** Clarify grouping and affiliation.

**Failure:** A camera view can accidentally group characters who are narratively separate.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: inspect projected spacing around fixed blocking rather than repositioning people merely to alter the group.

### P06 - Similarity

**Meaning:** Shared visible characteristics can imply relatedness. [S06](sources-and-cases.md#s06)

**Choose when:** Make a system or a deliberate exception readable.

**Failure:** An unintended shared colour/shape may create a false relationship.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: compare the permitted shape and value patterns while preserving exact approved costumes.

### P07 - Common region

**Meaning:** A visible boundary can group contained elements. [S08](sources-and-cases.md#s08)

**Choose when:** Use an existing doorway or enclosure to organise relationships.

**Failure:** A decorative border may imply a boundary absent from the story.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: find an approved opening that groups the relevant participants; do not invent a partition.

### P08 - Common fate

**Meaning:** Similar movement can be perceived as grouping. [S05](sources-and-cases.md#s05)

**Choose when:** Distinguish a moving group from a still participant.

**Failure:** A still cannot prove a motion effect, and crowds need not move in synchrony.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: video-only comparison of a purposeful shared route versus independently motivated background activity.

## 3. Space, composition and scale

These are composition mechanisms and proposed film applications, not a requirement to use a different device in every shot. Established world geometry stays fixed; perspective is selected around it.

### C01 - Frame within a frame

**Meaning:** An existing boundary creates a second visual enclosure. [S09](sources-and-cases.md#s09)

**Choose when:** Concentrate attention or express access/separation.

**Failure:** An opening can become ornament or hide a required face/contact.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: use a verified doorway while retaining the location's actual dimensions and the subject's approved position.

### C02 - Negative space

**Meaning:** Unoccupied visual area can carry directional or narrative purpose. [S05](sources-and-cases.md#s05)

**Choose when:** Support waiting, absence, a destination or withheld information.

**Failure:** Empty space without a task may simply weaken the frame.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: reserve an established entry region before an authorised arrival; no new person is required in the current still.

### C03 - Overlap

**Meaning:** One element partly covering another supplies depth/order cues. [S03](sources-and-cases.md#s03)

**Choose when:** Clarify foreground, action and background relationships.

**Failure:** Tangencies and occlusion can hide the story-bearing contact.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: layer relevant subjects while keeping the gesture or exchange readable.

### C04 - Scale disparity

**Meaning:** Expressive size relationships can emphasise meaning. [S09](sources-and-cases.md#s09)

**Choose when:** Communicate environmental dominance or relative visual weight.

**Failure:** Resizing a canonical body or prop breaks literal continuity.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: use feasible distance and framing around stable objects; reserve impossible scale for an authorised conceptual design.

### C05 - Diagonal organisation

**Meaning:** A diagonal can guide attention across a composition. [S03](sources-and-cases.md#s03)

**Choose when:** Use existing routes, stairs or body vectors.

**Failure:** A diagonal alone does not justify a Dutch roll.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: derive the image line from actual set geometry while keeping the camera level if the scene requires it.

### C06 - Symmetry

**Meaning:** Corresponding visual weight can organise a stable frame. [S04](sources-and-cases.md#s04)

**Choose when:** Establish ritual, order or a deliberate formal relationship.

**Failure:** Automatic centring can flatten a scene's changing power.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: preserve the geometry but let a supported gesture disturb the formal balance.

### C07 - Asymmetric balance

**Meaning:** Unequal placement can still produce deliberate balance. [S04](sources-and-cases.md#s04)

**Choose when:** Build unequal depth or a meaningful 2S/OTS relationship.

**Failure:** A random empty side is not intentional asymmetry.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: a relevant soft edge participant and a sharp principal subject, without inventing a third actor.

### C08 - Deep staging

**Meaning:** Several spatial planes carry necessary action or evidence; proposed film strategy.

**Choose when:** Show a relationship that a face-only crop cannot explain.

**Failure:** Foreground/background decoration can overload the read.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: retain two essential planes in adequate focus; shallow blur is optional, not the default answer to depth.

## 4. Materials, craft and graphic systems

A material is a visual and functional choice, not merely a texture preset. The following applications distinguish authored construction from accidental artefacts and protect literal-world continuity.

### M01 - Visible facture

**Meaning:** The maker's process remains perceptible in the result. [S09](sources-and-cases.md#s09)

**Choose when:** Give a chosen image an authored physical character.

**Failure:** Generic grain, dirt or AI errors do not establish craft.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: preserve intentional brushwork in an authorised painted set; avoid copying a reference artist's complete image.

### M02 - Material substitution

**Meaning:** Change expected material while preserving a recognisable form. [S24](sources-and-cases.md#s24)

**Choose when:** Explore a functional or sensory contradiction.

**Failure:** An unrequested material change mutates a literal prop.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: a conceptual object study; keep the production prop unchanged unless the design itself is authorised.

### M03 - Collage / recombination

**Meaning:** Fragments are composed into a new pictorial whole. [S03](sources-and-cases.md#s03)

**Choose when:** Create a graphic relationship across disparate elements.

**Failure:** A collage is not automatically coherent photographic space.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: choose deliberate edge treatment and hierarchy, then label the deliverable as editorial/graphic rather than a literal frame.

### M04 - Assemblage

**Meaning:** A three-dimensional work combines objects/materials. [S25](sources-and-cases.md#s25)

**Choose when:** Make physical relationships part of the concept.

**Failure:** A pile of unrelated objects supplies neither concept nor usable set dressing.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: develop a separate concept study from permitted objects; do not silently populate an existing film set.

### M05 - Visual quotation

**Meaning:** An image references a recognisable visual tradition. [S09](sources-and-cases.md#s09)

**Choose when:** Borrow a relationship or staging grammar for a new objective.

**Failure:** Recognition is audience-dependent; copying assets is a separate issue.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: study the compositional logic and replace the borrowed subject matter with the current brief's approved inventory.

### M06 - Typography as image

**Meaning:** Letterforms perform pictorial work. [S03](sources-and-cases.md#s03)

**Choose when:** Unite text and concept in a poster or title design.

**Failure:** Distortion can destroy legibility or invite unwanted text into a storyboard.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: verify actual Hindi shaping and thumbnail readability only when the deliverable requires lettering.

### M07 - Repeatable visual system

**Meaning:** Shared construction rules support variation. [S20](sources-and-cases.md#s20)

**Choose when:** Keep a campaign recognisable across formats.

**Failure:** Repeating one layout without adapting its read creates a rigid template.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: define invariant geometry/type roles and permitted variation rather than copying the same frame into every placement.

### M08 - Authored imperfection

**Meaning:** A deliberate human trace may support a contextual reading. [S10](sources-and-cases.md#s10)

**Choose when:** Express a personal or handmade communication.

**Failure:** Artificial errors may feel staged; imperfection is not proof of authenticity.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: compare a clean and a purposefully handwritten execution with intended readers, without declaring one universally superior.

## 5. Narrative and sequence strategies

These entries are proposed directing strategies and standard editing applications. They connect art direction to a film's time and causality; they are not additional compulsory Iris categories.

### T01 - Prop-led inference

**Meaning:** An existing object state supplies evidence; proposed strategy.

**Choose when:** Communicate choice, relationship or consequence through detail.

**Failure:** A symbolic insert may invent information or reveal it too early.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: show a supported, already established object after a meaningful handling change; preserve ownership and chronology.

### T02 - Environmental traces

**Meaning:** Use-specific details suggest a lived history; proposed transfer from design practice. [S22](sources-and-cases.md#s22)

**Choose when:** Build a plausible occupied world.

**Failure:** Uniform dirt stereotypes a place and ignores actual use.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: research the relevant household or workplace; assign each wear pattern a physical cause rather than an aesthetic adjective.

### T03 - Reveal / conceal

**Meaning:** Control when permitted information becomes visible; proposed strategy.

**Choose when:** Let a frame disclose a story fact at the intended moment.

**Failure:** Opaque framing that hides essential evidence damages clarity.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: use approved occlusion and camera scope; keep known offscreen positions consistent until the reveal.

### T04 - Graphic match

**Meaning:** Connect shots through a selected visual correspondence. [S18](sources-and-cases.md#s18)

**Choose when:** Link an idea across a cut.

**Failure:** Shape similarity without narrative purpose becomes a trick.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: record outgoing/incoming scale, placement and value; distinguish a suggested later shot from actually delivered coverage.

### T05 - Match on action

**Meaning:** Continue one action coherently across a cut. [S18](sources-and-cases.md#s18)

**Choose when:** Preserve physical causality while changing viewpoint.

**Failure:** Repeating the gesture's start or skipping contact breaks the event.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: map the exact action phase and retain limb/prop ownership at both ends.

### T06 - J/L sound bridge

**Meaning:** Incoming audio leads picture, or outgoing audio continues beyond its cut. [S18](sources-and-cases.md#s18)

**Choose when:** Carry a stimulus or relationship across images.

**Failure:** An offscreen line can accidentally animate the wrong visible mouth.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: identify source, onset and listener; keep audio-only sources out of visual reference bindings.

### T07 - Motif with development

**Meaning:** A recurring element gains a changed contextual reading; proposed strategy.

**Choose when:** Connect stages of an emotional arc.

**Failure:** Repeated decoration without changed significance creates redundancy.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: retain an approved object's design and vary its meaning through supported use, not unexplained visual mutation.

### T08 - Palette progression

**Meaning:** A planned colour relationship develops through a sequence; proposed strategy.

**Choose when:** Support a story transition where lighting/design changes are authorised.

**Failure:** Independent grading of each shot breaks continuity.

**Application / boundary:** Proposed: establish fixed anchors and allowed transitions; do not apply a symbolic colour change to a strict frame match.

## Selection rule

For a literal film scene, prefer mechanisms that can be achieved with approved inventory, camera, focus and action. Require explicit conceptual scope before material/scale transformations. A catalogue suggestion never overrides strict frames, identity, exact tags, world positions, action ownership or approved dialogue. Check depiction and intended meaning separately.
