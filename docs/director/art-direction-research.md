## 01. Findings, scope and evidence

Art direction ko ek decorative look-list ki tarah treat karne se important decisions chhoot jaate hain. Is study ka working model hai: intended meaning, believable world, selected image aur sequence ka relationship. Yeh model practical synthesis hai; ek universal official taxonomy nahi.

Study mein 25 references use hue: original scholarship, museum records, industry guild guidance, designers' project accounts, creator interviews aur professional teaching. Foundational research ko recent news nahi kaha gaya hai. Modern Art Direction ki public writing aur Iris preview dekhe gaye; restricted complete library/course inspect nahi hua. Reference photographs ko republish nahi kiya gaya. Report ke diagrams original explanatory schematics hain, generated film frames ya audience experiments nahi.

**Evidence labels:** source finding = linked author/institution kya kehta hai; working interpretation = term ko practical language mein translate karna; proposed application = is report ka exercise/workflow, abhi production-tested nahi. A critic's interpretation ko artist ki confirmed intention, aur a teaching heuristic ko measured scientific result mat samjho.

Main findings: a recognisable style does not by itself supply an idea; similar-looking images can use different mechanisms; different-looking images can share one mechanism; the required meaning can fail even when rendering is polished; a strong reference study records relationships rather than copying inventory; and a literal scene, poster, campaign and subjective image need different permissions and continuity checks.

The detailed catalog contains 40 non-exclusive techniques/strategies in five working families. Kuch established terms hain, kuch explicit practical strategies. It is not Iris's full taxonomy or a claim that art direction has exactly 40 types.

## 02. What art direction owns and coordinates

ScreenSkills describes screenplay-led visual-world development by the production designer and practical realisation by art directors, construction, dressing and related teams. The production designer works with the director and other visual departments. [S13, S14]

British Film Designers Guild separately describes campaign-focused commercials art direction and the practical/logistical film art-director role. The title can therefore mean different responsibilities in advertising and a large film crew. [S15]

APDG guidance emphasises early script analysis, research, location/studio choices, design documentation, budget priorities and coordination with other design/VFX work. Department boundaries depend on scale. [S16]

The following division is this report's proposed planning aid:

| Layer | Core decision | Concrete record |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Audience ko kya samajhna/feel karna hai? | One-sentence intent and intended inference |
| World | Region, period, lived use, character context | Researched world brief; facts vs proposals |
| Physical design | Architecture, surfaces, dressing, props | Layout, scale, material and ownership register |
| Image | Viewpoint, framing, focus and light | Shot-local visual design |
| Sequence | Causality, reveal and emotional rhythm | Entry/exit states and cut connections |
| Execution | Can the chosen image actually be produced? | Reference roles, tool constraints and inspected output |

**Style and mechanism are different axes.** Naturalistic, stylized, minimal, maximal, theatrical and painterly describe approaches to appearance. Period, contemporary, fantasy and future describe worlds/time settings. Metaphor, substitution, grouping and visual hierarchy describe how an image communicates. A film may be period, naturalistic and sparse while using a subtle visual opposition. A graphic ad may use a surreal fusion within a very minimal layout.

## 03. How an image makes meaning

Phillips and McQuarrie propose a visual-rhetoric framework combining three structures with three meaning operations. Structures are juxtaposition, fusion and replacement; operations concern connection, similarity and opposition. Their cross-product yields nine configurations. These are proposed distinctions and predictions in pictorial advertising, not proof that more ambiguity always improves an image. [S01]

| Structure | Connection | Similarity | Opposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Juxtaposition | Separate elements associated | Separate elements compared | Separate elements contrasted |
| Fusion | Association in a combined form | Composite invites likeness | Composite invites contradiction |
| Replacement | Missing associate inferred | Missing counterpart inferred | Missing contrast inferred |

**Proposed concept test:** name the target idea, the source analogy and the exact shared relationship. Then ask whether the viewer has enough visible context to infer that relationship. Replacing an object because its shape fits is only a formal operation; the brief still needs a communicative reason.

Example exercise: a campaign about protecting time may explore a calendar and a shelter as separate images, a composite form, or an implied missing element. These are proposed conceptual directions. They are not a production instruction to add a calendar, shelter or surreal transformation to an existing scene.

Iris publicly lists form substitution, contextual metaphor, semantic compounding, pop-out and silhouette, and describes its readings as context-dependent and editable. The public preview does not provide a complete verified dictionary. [S02] Treat the catalog's V04-V05 descriptions as working interpretations; do not claim proprietary equivalence to academic terms.

## 04. Meaning and visual rhetoric

Working explanations below combine established vocabulary and explicit interpretations. V04-V05 are operational readings, not verified full Iris definitions. The academic replacement category is not identical to every physical object swap.

### V01 - Juxtaposition

**Meaning:** Distinct elements shown together invite a relationship. [S01]

**Useful when:** Show two relevant states for comparison.

**Failure:** Two unrelated attractive objects provide no interpretable connection.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: an approved worn work tool beside its unused pristine counterpart, when that contrast serves the brief.

### V02 - Fusion

**Meaning:** Two recognisable domains share one composite form. [S01]

**Useful when:** Compress a comparison into a campaign image.

**Failure:** A hybrid can look inventive while obscuring both source concepts.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed conceptual exercise: a calendar whose pages form a protective enclosure; explain the intended protection-of-time relation.

### V03 - Replacement

**Meaning:** One shown element prompts inference of another absent element. [S01]

**Useful when:** Let context supply a missing concept.

**Failure:** Showing both elements may change the intended structure to juxtaposition or fusion.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: replace an expected component in a familiar setting while keeping enough context for viewers to infer its usual role.

### V04 - Contextual metaphor

**Meaning:** Unexpected use or setting suggests a second meaning; working interpretation. [S02]

**Useful when:** Make a familiar item carry a new idea.

**Failure:** Random incongruity becomes a puzzle without a useful intended reading.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: a permitted object treated according to another object's ritual of use, only in an explicitly conceptual image.

### V05 - Semantic compounding

**Meaning:** Several meanings operate together; exact restricted Iris entry remains unverified. [S02]

**Useful when:** Explore a compact image with a purposeful second reading.

**Failure:** Multiple possible readings can defeat the required message.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: write both intended readings first, then check whether the visible evidence supports each independently.

### V06 - Metonymy

**Meaning:** A part or associated element stands for a larger whole. [S01]

**Useful when:** Communicate through a meaningful existing detail.

**Failure:** A generic detail may identify neither character nor situation.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: an established working hand and tool can imply labour; it cannot identify an unseen person without sufficient context.

### V07 - Rebus

**Meaning:** Pictures substitute for spoken sounds or letters. [S21]

**Useful when:** Create a language-dependent graphic message.

**Failure:** The wordplay may fail in a different language or culture.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: test a Hindi sound-based design with Hindi readers instead of assuming an English rebus translates.

### V08 - Opposition / visual irony

**Meaning:** Visible elements invite a meaningful difference or contradiction. [S01]

**Useful when:** Expose a gap between appearance and claim.

**Failure:** The viewer may infer similarity when opposition was intended.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: contrast an authorised claim of order with already established visible disorder; do not invent incriminating evidence.

## 05. Attention and perceptual grouping

The references here largely teach visual/interface design. Their application to a film frame is a proposed transfer. They describe perceptual tendencies, not a guarantee that all viewers interpret an image identically.

### P01 - Visual hierarchy

**Meaning:** Order attention through relative emphasis. [S04]

**Useful when:** Establish first read and essential secondary evidence.

**Failure:** A large bright background feature can outrank the intended expression.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: judge the frame at delivery size before adding detail to its least important area.

### P02 - Contrast / pop-out

**Meaning:** A visible difference makes an element distinguishable. [S04]

**Useful when:** Separate a relevant detail through value, colour, size or orientation.

**Failure:** Too many competing differences create several accidental focal points.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: keep one critical gesture legible against an existing quieter background rather than adding a new spotlight.

### P03 - Figure-ground

**Meaning:** Perception organises a subject relative to its surrounding field. [S05]

**Useful when:** Maintain a readable outline and subject relationship.

**Failure:** Subject and background can merge at the decisive contact or silhouette.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: change an eligible camera position to separate a hand from a similar-value surface without moving its owner.

### P04 - Closure

**Meaning:** A partial shape can suggest a complete object. [S05]

**Useful when:** Use controlled omission when recognition is sufficient.

**Failure:** Cropping necessary evidence is not purposeful closure.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: test recognisability without showing every contour; retain full information for a required hand transfer.

### P05 - Proximity

**Meaning:** Near elements tend to be read as related. [S07]

**Useful when:** Clarify grouping and affiliation.

**Failure:** A camera view can accidentally group characters who are narratively separate.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: inspect projected spacing around fixed blocking rather than repositioning people merely to alter the group.

### P06 - Similarity

**Meaning:** Shared visible characteristics can imply relatedness. [S06]

**Useful when:** Make a system or a deliberate exception readable.

**Failure:** An unintended shared colour/shape may create a false relationship.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: compare the permitted shape and value patterns while preserving exact approved costumes.

### P07 - Common region

**Meaning:** A visible boundary can group contained elements. [S08]

**Useful when:** Use an existing doorway or enclosure to organise relationships.

**Failure:** A decorative border may imply a boundary absent from the story.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: find an approved opening that groups the relevant participants; do not invent a partition.

### P08 - Common fate

**Meaning:** Similar movement can be perceived as grouping. [S05]

**Useful when:** Distinguish a moving group from a still participant.

**Failure:** A still cannot prove a motion effect, and crowds need not move in synchrony.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: video-only comparison of a purposeful shared route versus independently motivated background activity.

## 06. Space, composition and scale

These are composition mechanisms and proposed film applications, not a requirement to use a different device in every shot. Established world geometry stays fixed; perspective is selected around it.

### S01 - Frame within a frame

**Meaning:** An existing boundary creates a second visual enclosure. [S09]

**Useful when:** Concentrate attention or express access/separation.

**Failure:** An opening can become ornament or hide a required face/contact.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: use a verified doorway while retaining the location's actual dimensions and the subject's approved position.

### S02 - Negative space

**Meaning:** Unoccupied visual area can carry directional or narrative purpose. [S05]

**Useful when:** Support waiting, absence, a destination or withheld information.

**Failure:** Empty space without a task may simply weaken the frame.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: reserve an established entry region before an authorised arrival; no new person is required in the current still.

### S03 - Overlap

**Meaning:** One element partly covering another supplies depth/order cues. [S03]

**Useful when:** Clarify foreground, action and background relationships.

**Failure:** Tangencies and occlusion can hide the story-bearing contact.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: layer relevant subjects while keeping the gesture or exchange readable.

### S04 - Scale disparity

**Meaning:** Expressive size relationships can emphasise meaning. [S09]

**Useful when:** Communicate environmental dominance or relative visual weight.

**Failure:** Resizing a canonical body or prop breaks literal continuity.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: use feasible distance and framing around stable objects; reserve impossible scale for an authorised conceptual design.

### S05 - Diagonal organisation

**Meaning:** A diagonal can guide attention across a composition. [S03]

**Useful when:** Use existing routes, stairs or body vectors.

**Failure:** A diagonal alone does not justify a Dutch roll.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: derive the image line from actual set geometry while keeping the camera level if the scene requires it.

### S06 - Symmetry

**Meaning:** Corresponding visual weight can organise a stable frame. [S04]

**Useful when:** Establish ritual, order or a deliberate formal relationship.

**Failure:** Automatic centring can flatten a scene's changing power.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: preserve the geometry but let a supported gesture disturb the formal balance.

### S07 - Asymmetric balance

**Meaning:** Unequal placement can still produce deliberate balance. [S04]

**Useful when:** Build unequal depth or a meaningful 2S/OTS relationship.

**Failure:** A random empty side is not intentional asymmetry.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: a relevant soft edge participant and a sharp principal subject, without inventing a third actor.

### S08 - Deep staging

**Meaning:** Several spatial planes carry necessary action or evidence; proposed film strategy.

**Useful when:** Show a relationship that a face-only crop cannot explain.

**Failure:** Foreground/background decoration can overload the read.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: retain two essential planes in adequate focus; shallow blur is optional, not the default answer to depth.

## 07. Materials, craft and graphic systems

A material is a visual and functional choice, not merely a texture preset. The following applications distinguish authored construction from accidental artefacts and protect literal-world continuity.

### M01 - Visible facture

**Meaning:** The maker's process remains perceptible in the result. [S09]

**Useful when:** Give a chosen image an authored physical character.

**Failure:** Generic grain, dirt or AI errors do not establish craft.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: preserve intentional brushwork in an authorised painted set; avoid copying a reference artist's complete image.

### M02 - Material substitution

**Meaning:** Change expected material while preserving a recognisable form. [S24]

**Useful when:** Explore a functional or sensory contradiction.

**Failure:** An unrequested material change mutates a literal prop.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: a conceptual object study; keep the production prop unchanged unless the design itself is authorised.

### M03 - Collage / recombination

**Meaning:** Fragments are composed into a new pictorial whole. [S03]

**Useful when:** Create a graphic relationship across disparate elements.

**Failure:** A collage is not automatically coherent photographic space.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: choose deliberate edge treatment and hierarchy, then label the deliverable as editorial/graphic rather than a literal frame.

### M04 - Assemblage

**Meaning:** A three-dimensional work combines objects/materials. [S25]

**Useful when:** Make physical relationships part of the concept.

**Failure:** A pile of unrelated objects supplies neither concept nor usable set dressing.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: develop a separate concept study from permitted objects; do not silently populate an existing film set.

### M05 - Visual quotation

**Meaning:** An image references a recognisable visual tradition. [S09]

**Useful when:** Borrow a relationship or staging grammar for a new objective.

**Failure:** Recognition is audience-dependent; copying assets is a separate issue.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: study the compositional logic and replace the borrowed subject matter with the current brief's approved inventory.

### M06 - Typography as image

**Meaning:** Letterforms perform pictorial work. [S03]

**Useful when:** Unite text and concept in a poster or title design.

**Failure:** Distortion can destroy legibility or invite unwanted text into a storyboard.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: verify actual Hindi shaping and thumbnail readability only when the deliverable requires lettering.

### M07 - Repeatable visual system

**Meaning:** Shared construction rules support variation. [S20]

**Useful when:** Keep a campaign recognisable across formats.

**Failure:** Repeating one layout without adapting its read creates a rigid template.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: define invariant geometry/type roles and permitted variation rather than copying the same frame into every placement.

### M08 - Authored imperfection

**Meaning:** A deliberate human trace may support a contextual reading. [S10]

**Useful when:** Express a personal or handmade communication.

**Failure:** Artificial errors may feel staged; imperfection is not proof of authenticity.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: compare a clean and a purposefully handwritten execution with intended readers, without declaring one universally superior.

## 08. Narrative and sequence strategies

These entries are proposed directing strategies and standard editing applications. They connect art direction to a film's time and causality; they are not additional compulsory Iris categories.

### T01 - Prop-led inference

**Meaning:** An existing object state supplies evidence; proposed strategy.

**Useful when:** Communicate choice, relationship or consequence through detail.

**Failure:** A symbolic insert may invent information or reveal it too early.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: show a supported, already established object after a meaningful handling change; preserve ownership and chronology.

### T02 - Environmental traces

**Meaning:** Use-specific details suggest a lived history; proposed transfer from design practice. [S22]

**Useful when:** Build a plausible occupied world.

**Failure:** Uniform dirt stereotypes a place and ignores actual use.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: research the relevant household or workplace; assign each wear pattern a physical cause rather than an aesthetic adjective.

### T03 - Reveal / conceal

**Meaning:** Control when permitted information becomes visible; proposed strategy.

**Useful when:** Let a frame disclose a story fact at the intended moment.

**Failure:** Opaque framing that hides essential evidence damages clarity.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: use approved occlusion and camera scope; keep known offscreen positions consistent until the reveal.

### T04 - Graphic match

**Meaning:** Connect shots through a selected visual correspondence. [S18]

**Useful when:** Link an idea across a cut.

**Failure:** Shape similarity without narrative purpose becomes a trick.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: record outgoing/incoming scale, placement and value; distinguish a suggested later shot from actually delivered coverage.

### T05 - Match on action

**Meaning:** Continue one action coherently across a cut. [S18]

**Useful when:** Preserve physical causality while changing viewpoint.

**Failure:** Repeating the gesture's start or skipping contact breaks the event.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: map the exact action phase and retain limb/prop ownership at both ends.

### T06 - J/L sound bridge

**Meaning:** Incoming audio leads picture, or outgoing audio continues beyond its cut. [S18]

**Useful when:** Carry a stimulus or relationship across images.

**Failure:** An offscreen line can accidentally animate the wrong visible mouth.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: identify source, onset and listener; keep audio-only sources out of visual reference bindings.

### T07 - Motif with development

**Meaning:** A recurring element gains a changed contextual reading; proposed strategy.

**Useful when:** Connect stages of an emotional arc.

**Failure:** Repeated decoration without changed significance creates redundancy.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: retain an approved object's design and vary its meaning through supported use, not unexplained visual mutation.

### T08 - Palette progression

**Meaning:** A planned colour relationship develops through a sequence; proposed strategy.

**Useful when:** Support a story transition where lighting/design changes are authorised.

**Failure:** Independent grading of each shot breaks continuity.

**Example / boundary:** Proposed: establish fixed anchors and allowed transitions; do not apply a symbolic colour change to a strict frame match.

## 09. Eight source-grounded case studies

The source facts below are deliberately brief. Transfers and boundaries are this report's proposed applications, not confirmed creator intentions or measured audience outcomes. Read the linked originals for the actual works.

### Case 1 - Parasite: space can carry the drama

**Source finding:** In the 2019 interview, Bong connects the semi-basement condition with the family's unstable hope; Lee describes researching inhabited traces and arranging the sets around cinematic needs. [S22]

**Proposed transfer:** map levels, access, sightlines and routes before camera selection. Let the required action be possible in the set. Research ordinary use rather than adding generic clutter.

**Boundary:** this does not mean every poor home must be dark or dirty. Borrow the problem-solving method, not the Korean house, its plot or its cultural coding. No whole-film pixel/shot-by-shot analysis was performed here.

### Case 2 - Her: a future world can be emotionally specific

**Source finding:** The Academy describes the film's near-future visual concept and Barrett's combination of Los Angeles and Shanghai's Pudong skyline. [S23]

**Proposed transfer:** start with the emotional and social conditions of the future, then choose design changes that support them. A future setting need not default to neon interfaces, glossy metal or hostile blue light.

**Boundary:** those are alternative design possibilities, not claims about every set in the film. Do not import a sci-fi look into a locked contemporary village.

### Case 3 - MIT Media Lab: a system is more than a logo

**Source finding:** Pentagram developed an ML monogram and related marks for 23 research groups from the same seven-by-seven grid used by the earlier identity. The designer's account describes fixed identity alongside variation. [S20]

**Proposed transfer:** define what stays invariant and what may change across posters, episodes or touchpoints. A repeatable geometry can create family resemblance without requiring identical layouts.

**Boundary:** a consistent series is not proof of comprehension, market performance or originality. Do not copy the actual glyphs into a new brand.

### Case 4 - EYE-BEE-M: recognition depends on language

**Source finding:** UMMA describes Paul Rand's use of an eye and bee to substitute for the sounds/letters I and B, alongside the striped M. Its record concerns a particular edition. [S21]

**Proposed transfer:** test pictorial wordplay in the intended language. Separate object recognition from the sound/word inference required by the rebus.

**Boundary:** do not assume English soundplay survives translation into Hindi. Also distinguish an edition's collection date from a design's first creation date.

### Case 5 - Oppenheim's Object: material can change function

**Source finding:** MoMA records the 1936 fur-covered cup, saucer and spoon and discusses the conflicting tactile and drinking associations. [S24]

**Proposed transfer:** ask what a material makes an object invite, resist or suggest. Material choice can do conceptual work even when the silhouette stays familiar.

**Boundary:** this is an art-object example. Replacing a brass production prop's material without permission would be a continuity failure, not conceptual sophistication.

### Case 6 - Mako analysis: study ingredients, not a copied look

**Source finding:** Murray's analysis identifies visible facture, visual quotation, nested framing and size contrasts as recurring ingredients. This is the critic's reading of the work. [S09]

**Proposed transfer:** annotate a reference by relationship, evidence and attention mechanism. Recombine those mechanisms for a different brief rather than copying a photographer's subject, set and palette together.

**Boundary:** a material-looking AI artefact is not automatically intentional facture. Nor does the analysis independently verify the maker's complete process.

### Case 7 - Polaroid commentary: a small choice can anchor a reading

**Source finding:** Murray interprets blue handwriting in the campaign as a personal, human trace and contrasts it with a typeset treatment. [S10]

**Proposed transfer:** identify the one execution detail carrying the intended association, then compare whether another treatment changes the message.

**Boundary:** blue ink is not universal proof of authenticity. The critic's contract/ink assertions are not adopted as legal facts or scientific colour rules. No campaign conversion or trust lift was verified.

### Case 8 - Milkbar feature: the medium can continue the world

**Source finding:** The guest feature describes a locally inflected imagined past and a newspaper execution, with founder comments about period printing/paper choices and physical distribution. [S11]

**Proposed transfer:** an art-direction idea can influence format and distribution, not only a photograph's grade. Test whether the chosen touchpoint makes sense for the audience and concept.

**Boundary:** a reported campaign is not an experiment proving offline promotion works better. Cultural references and nostalgia require local reading, not universal assumptions.

## 10. Colour, materials and a believable world

Elliot's 2015 review stresses that colour meaning and psychological effects depend on context and methodological conditions. It discusses hue, lightness and chroma, and cautions against premature general application. This is a foundational review, not an exhaustive account of all later research. [S19]

**Proposed colour worksheet:** record the narrative role of a colour; which existing surface/object owns it; its relative value/chroma; the motivating illumination; relevant costume/skin/material relationships; and what remains fixed across shots. Separate source colour, illumination and finishing grade. A bright warm frame can still carry grief; a dark frame need not imply evil.

Adobe's practical compositing guidance requires compatible perspective, illumination, shadows and colour for photographic integration, even when the depicted scene is imagined. [S17]

| Material question | What to inspect | Proposed failure check |
|---|---|---|
| What is it made of? | Reflectance, translucency, weave/grain and edge behaviour | Generic texture pasted onto an incompatible form |
| How is it used? | Grip, contact, load, cleaning and repair | Wear placed where no handling or weathering occurs |
| What is its scale? | Hand/body relationship and support | A bag or idol grows between angles |
| What light reaches it? | Source direction, highlights, shadow contact and bounce | New highlight suggests a relocated source |
| What does it communicate? | Evidence in this story and culture | A stereotype substituted for research |

Avoid universal equations such as gold = divine, red = anger, blue = authentic or old = dirty. Use the brief and actual context. Where the user has an approved palette, keep it; do not use this research as permission to recolour a strict reference.

### Set research that produces decisions

The following is a proposed research process. Define place and period; collect relevant first-hand or institutional evidence; distinguish original construction from later repairs; identify the inhabitant's work and routines; map essential architecture; choose useful dressing; and test it from planned cameras. Record unknowns rather than inventing them. A visually rich room should still have believable entrances, support and routes.

For an Indian rural setting, research the actual region and household/workplace rather than substituting a generic collection of clay pots, cracked walls and warm filters. Sources should support the architecture, materials and lived use. Never transfer one project's location tag or costume rule into another.

## 11. From research to a filmable sequence

Proposed working order: source/intent, character action, stable geography, visual mechanism, camera/depth/focus, motivated light, selected still instant, motion/edit/sound, and output review. This is an operational proposal informed by the research, not a measured quality result.

**World first:** mark fixed entrances, levels, seats, prop support and character relationships. A reverse view changes projected screen placement; it does not relocate those anchors. **Image next:** choose which evidence must share a frame and which can be separated across cuts. **Sequence last:** check that later frames inherit the prior physical and emotional state.

<!-- DIAGRAM: WORLD -->

Art direction supplies the physical world; cinematography selects and lights a view of it; editing connects those views. Real productions coordinate these decisions rather than treating departments as isolated. A reflected view, silhouette or foreground occlusion needs valid ownership and geometry.

Adobe describes match cuts and sound-led J/L transitions and highlights continuity through action, eyelines and timing. [S18] In this report's proposed use, log the outgoing/incoming shared cue and story function. A graphic match across two different events is not the same as continuing one action. A sound bridge is a separate audio relationship, and a still cannot demonstrate its timing.

### Detailed coverage without filler

For each meaningful beat ask: what triggers it; who perceives it; what is processed; what visible response follows; what changes; what may remain hidden? Choose close reactions, hand/object inserts, a spatial master or a held frame only when they carry that work. Quiet processing is a valid event. An insert has to show evidence, not simply prove that a prop exists.

## 12. Synthetic exercise: an emotionally complete nine-shot plan

This is an original teaching exercise, not a supplied screenplay, production plan or new project canon. No registered tags/assets have been assigned. Role labels below must be mapped to real approved identifiers before any executable prompt or generation.

**Exercise brief:** A repairer closes a workshop for good. An established repaired clock, wrapping paper and a key are on the counter. A regular customer enters to collect the clock. The repairer wraps and hands it over; the customer leaves. The repairer turns the CLOSED sign and locks the door. The intended feeling is quiet pride mixed with loss. Layout: counter near the window, one entrance and a clear route between them.

| Shot | Purpose and selected instant | Local inventory / continuity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Master: repairer at the counter before the visitor arrives | Repairer, needed counter/clock context, relevant interior view; visitor absent |
| 2 | Insert: fingers crease the paper around the supported clock | Repairer hand owner, clock, paper and counter surface; no face/outfit list |
| 3 | Arrival: customer pauses in the established doorway | Customer and actual doorway view; do not add the repairer outside the crop |
| 4 | Shared frame: first stable contact during the handover | Both owners, wrapped clock, real exchange space and readable hands |
| 5 | Customer reaction after receiving it | Customer, supported parcel if visible, matching local background; cause precedes response |
| 6 | Repairer holds a restrained response | Repairer only where visible; emotional state carries from the exchange |
| 7 | Insert: customer's supported grip settles on the parcel before leaving | Customer hand owner, parcel, necessary surface/background; no unrelated cast |
| 8 | After the departure: repairer turns the existing CLOSED sign | Repairer hand owner, sign and actual door view; exit already completed |
| 9 | Ending: key meets the lock as the door is secured | Repairer hand owner, key, lock and door surface; contact and orientation stay coherent |

**Proposed variants:** compare a shared-depth handover with separated close coverage; compare private processing in a clean single versus a relevant dirty foreground. Keep the same world arrangement and source inventory. If a later approved sequence includes a clock dial, a key-turn/dial graphic connection can be considered as an alternative destination; it is not automatically an extra scene or delivered frame.

**Scope lesson:** the full workshop story guides planning, but each shot receives only its own necessary information. A detailed request increases relevant precision. It does not make the empty shelves, customer, key, sign and clock appear in every crop.

## 13. Templates for development and review

These are proposed reusable worksheets. They are not automated tests and their use does not establish audience acceptance.

### A. Art-direction brief

- Objective, audience, viewing context and delivery ratio.
- Source facts, approved changes and remaining unknowns.
- Emotional progression and intended first/second read.
- Region, period, inhabitant, routines and relevant research.
- World anchors, materials, dressing/prop roles and exact asset versions.
- Candidate visual mechanisms, their meaning and why they fit this deliverable.
- Lighting/palette invariants and authorised variables.
- Execution route, review evidence and unresolved requirements.

### B. Reference analysis card

- Source, creator/institution, date and what was actually inspected.
- Visible observation, separated from interpretation of intention.
- Primary subject/evidence and attention order.
- Spatial, graphic or semantic relationship doing the work.
- Materials, surface character, light and crop constraints.
- What can transfer as a method; what must remain with the original work.
- Alternative reading, cultural/language dependency and failure case.

### C. Shot-local compilation card

- Shot ID and one exact instant or authorised video progression.
- Required visible identities/partials, props and matching registered location view.
- Necessary pose/contact, expression, focus, camera and light.
- Required audio role, kept separate from visual inventory.
- Entry/exit continuity kept internally; exact dialogue last when applicable.
- Removal pass for absent subjects, off-frame traits, adjacent beats and scene-wide preambles.

Self-contained means enough for this shot, not a copy of the whole scene. Keep exact tags and genuine provenance. A hand-owner tag does not require the whole body onscreen. Do not invent view suffixes. Strict source-frame locks override optional symbolism or composition experiments.

### D. Cut connection card

- Outgoing shot and its exact action/attention state.
- Incoming shot and the corresponding state or deliberate contrast.
- Shared shape, placement, movement, sound or meaning.
- Narrative/emotional function and time/location boundary.
- Required readability, reaction onset and edit handles.
- Status: proposed connection, inspected edit or unresolved counterpart.

## 14. What to test before adopting a technique

Modern Art Direction's education article argues for demonstrating conceptual thinking through a completed spec campaign. Treat that as an educator's approach, not an employment guarantee. [S12] The following is this report's proposed small practice programme:

1. Same brief, three rhetorical structures: compare what meaning remains clear and what has changed.
2. Same blocking, several camera relationships: check ownership, scale and world anchors rather than only attractiveness.
3. Same message, clean versus dense layout: assess attention at actual delivery size.
4. Same object, a different permitted material: identify the intended functional/sensory change.
5. Same campaign mechanism across ratios: redesign hierarchy rather than blindly centre-cropping.
6. Same emotional beat, literal versus conceptual execution: keep them in separate deliverable modes.
7. Same visual idea, intended-language readers: check inference instead of assuming cultural universality.

Record brief, reference version, changed variable, expected effect, output and actual review. Hold approved facts/identity/geometry fixed during comparisons. Do not report a percentage gain from a handful of preferred images.

| Review dimension | Concrete question | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Depiction | Are the required things/actions present and correctly related? | Inspected image/sequence against source |
| Meaning | What does a viewer infer, without being told the intended answer? | Reader/viewer response, with context recorded |
| Coherence | Do perspective, support, scale and light work together? | Actual visual inspection |
| Sequence | Does action, emotion and reveal timing carry across shots? | Full relevant motion/edit review |
| Binding | Are exact required assets/roles/tags used locally? | Source/input comparison, not image similarity alone |
| Delivery | Does the idea survive the target ratio and size? | Actual exported format and viewing-size check |

No such audience or model-generation comparison was performed in this research. The original diagrams are teaching illustrations, not empirical validation. Findings can inform candidate designs; claims of better generated fidelity, emotion, retention or conversion remain pending until relevant evaluation.

## 15. Boundaries and next useful work

The public source study and this synthesis are complete for the present scope. Full Iris/course entries remain inaccessible, so no complete proprietary taxonomy is claimed. Some sources are professional teaching or interpretive case studies rather than controlled experiments; the distinctions are recorded in the source list.

The useful next work is a bounded design exercise on an actual approved brief: keep story and assets fixed, compare a few meaningfully distinct designs, inspect the outputs, and retain only the techniques that help that task. Broad experimentation and scene-specific relevance can coexist. Research does not authorise new plot, props, location tags, faces, costumes, lighting changes, paid generation or unrequested scenes.

This report contains no measured generation gains, paid-course extraction, or claim to have retrained the underlying model. Existing approved frame-local prompting and continuity rules stay in force. Sourced knowledge and proposed applications remain distinguishable.
