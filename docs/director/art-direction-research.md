# Art direction: public research reference

Research and repository sync: 2026-11-22. Educational material, not new project canon or measured generation quality. The owner's composition/shot rules live in [Director rules](../DIRECTOR_RULES.md).

## Meaning, style and execution

Keep four questions distinct: what must be communicated; which visual relationship communicates it; what surface/style suits it; and how the image will be executed coherently. This is a working synthesis, not a universal taxonomy. A photographic film scene, an editorial metaphor and a graphic campaign need different treatments.

## Visual rhetoric

Phillips and McQuarrie's 2004 paper proposes three image structures: juxtaposition (distinct elements together), fusion (combined form) and replacement (a shown element invites inference of an absent one). It crosses these with association, comparison by similarity and comparison by opposition, producing nine proposed configurations. The paper develops testable predictions; it is not proof that greater complexity always improves communication. Its primary setting is pictorial advertising; film application needs separate judgment.

Primary paper: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1470593104044089

Author-uploaded text inspected: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235361038_Beyond_Visual_Metaphor_A_New_Typology_of_Visual_Rhetoric_in_Advertising

## Iris: observed names versus unverified definitions

The public Iris preview names form substitution, contextual metaphor, semantic compounding, pop-out and silhouette. It describes a contextual, editable reading of images. The complete restricted library was not inspected; no exhaustive technique count or exact proprietary definition is claimed.

For practical exploration, consider shape/component substitution, unexpected contextual use, combined meanings, perceptual distinctiveness and readable outlines. These are working interpretations. Do not assume semantic compounding equals academic fusion, or every object swap equals rhetorical replacement.

Public preview: https://visuals.modernartdirection.com/home

## Perception and attention

NN/g's visual-design material covers scale, hierarchy, balance, contrast and Gestalt organisation. Its glossary includes figure-ground, proximity, similarity, common region, connectedness, continuation, closure and common fate. These are primarily interface/visual-design references. Applying them to film is a proposed transfer, not a guarantee of identical audience response.

Use the ideas to ask: what is noticed first; what is grouped; what is inferred; and what competes with essential evidence? Similar motion can imply grouping in video; a still alone does not demonstrate that motion. Never turn an interface-specific size or font rule into a compulsory film-shot formula.

Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/ and https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-design-cheat-sheet/

## Making an authored visual world

Modern Art Direction's Makó analysis discusses visible facture (maker-visible surfaces/process), visual quotation, nested framing and expressive size contrasts. Its useful lesson is to study the mechanism and combine it for a new objective rather than copy a reference's subject or whole look. Adobe's compositing guidance adds an execution check: perspective, illumination, shadows and colour must work together when a photographic result is intended, including imagined scenes.

Sources: https://newsletter.modernartdirection.com/posts/the-art-direction-of-szilveszter-mako-mad-011 and https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/photography/discover/photo-manipulation.html

## Posters and campaigns

Cooper Hewitt's poster framework includes selective emphasis, deliberate density, pictorial lettering, layering/recombination, altered surfaces, reduction, implied events, urgency, multiple readings, expressive size differences, diagonals, gaze and repeatable systems. It is a curatorial classification, not the complete universe of art direction.

Modern Art Direction also discusses brand devices used as narrative frames, conceptual metaphors extended across executions, and the making process shown in the final creative. These are options, not instructions to add logos, captions, symbolic props or behind-the-scenes crews to every film frame.

Sources: https://www.cooperhewitt.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Final-How-Posters-Work-Release-4-30-15.pdf and https://newsletter.modernartdirection.com/posts/steal-these-art-direction-techniques-mad-005

## Production design

ScreenSkills describes the production designer's screenplay-led visual-world development and the art director's practical coordination of that vision through sets, construction and dressing. Department boundaries vary by scale. For assistant-led filmmaking, resolve region/period, world geometry, materials, useful objects, approved character/look records and camera/light interaction without conflating them.

Sources: https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/film-and-tv-drama/craft/production-designer-film-and-tv-drama/ and https://www.screenskills.com/skills-checklists/scripted-film-and-tv/art-department/art-director-skills/

## Proposed selection method and limits

Write the intended meaning; identify audience, viewing size/time and deliverable; compare meaningful visual mechanisms; select an executable design; then review meaning and appearance separately. For film, add causal/reveal timing and continuity checks. Compile each shot from its own required inventory under the Director rules. A beautiful image can still express the wrong relationship.

No new images, audience tests, temporal tests or model benchmarks were run in this research. Full Iris/course content remains unavailable. These public findings are a reference for useful experimentation, not permission to overwrite locked identity, lighting, tags, blocking or story. A 2026 visual-metaphor benchmark surfaced in search, but direct primary-page retrieval failed; its numerical/model claims are excluded.
