# Lighting choices for composition exploration

Use for new composition design and substantive reframing, alongside [Director rules](../DIRECTOR_RULES.md). Source of the standing exploration policy: Manik's direct 18 November 2026 instruction following the composition and lighting lists. This reference is a selection vocabulary, not a finite list of exclusive lighting types, a model-control guarantee or evidence of generated-media improvement.

## Scope before experimentation

- Open new scene/look: explore plausible sources and design alternatives from the scene's time, location, materials, emotion and approved style. Keep new design proposals distinct from canon.
- Established scene: retain source world direction, time, colour family, exposure character and approved visible practicals. A reverse camera changes how existing light reads, not where the sun or lamp is. Do not independently relight every storyboard cell.
- Strict frame or `fc/fm`: preserve actual source lighting unless the user explicitly includes lighting in the change. Composition experimentation alone is not a relighting instruction.
- Match the beat: essential expressions, hands, contacts and props must stay readable. A villain need not always be half-dark; a tender scene need not always be golden. Select from performance and visual intention.

## Make an experiment executable

Resolve `motivating source + world direction + shadow softness + contrast/fill + colour relationship + subject/background separation + continuity`. Keep only the fields needed to reproduce the selected shot; do not paste the entire library into prompts. Equipment names are optional production descriptions, not proof a generator simulates a fixture.

For useful comparison, vary one major relationship first, then combine compatible choices. Compare what changes in attention, facial/prop readability, emotional interpretation and the adjacent cut. Distinguish source colour from overall grading. Negative fill removes reflected light; it is not a lamp. Soft light can have strong contrast, and low-key does not require unreadable exposure. Hard/soft depends on apparent source size at the subject; diffusion or bounce must actually create a suitable effective source.

Do not introduce a lamp, fire, water, smoke, crowd or visible beam merely to use a named effect. New environmental effects need story support and authorization in a locked scene. A still contains one visible lighting state; describe flicker, sweeps or flashing progression only for motion work. Respect available space, surfaces, source distance/falloff and material response. Do not claim exact Kelvin/lux, measured ratios, temporal consistency or fixture controls without appropriate evidence.

Example: an unlocked afternoon tree scene may compare hard leaf patterns against softer sky/bounce fill while retaining the sun direction. For a strict supplied frame, explore only permitted crop/perspective/focus changes; preserve its existing face shadows, warmth and highlight character. For an unlocked night practical scene, compare plausible lantern/window sources; never carry those alternatives into the daytime continuity.
